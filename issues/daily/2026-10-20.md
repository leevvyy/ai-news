### 1 · DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the inference framework behind its production service, with Nvidia and Ascend support

`I = 76.1` · Models & labs · China · DeepSeek, Huawei Ascend, Nvidia

> 原标题：DeepSeek：进一步将内部推理框架 DeepInfer 全面开源

DeepSeek published DeepInfer, the inference framework it says powers its large-scale production serving, under Apache-2.0 on GitHub, plus a token encode/decode component, deepseek-recipe (reported MIT). The README lists DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash as the only supported model and targets Nvidia SM100 GPUs and Huawei Ascend 950 NPUs. Features include attention data parallelism, expert parallelism with EPLB, speculative decoding, sliding-window-attention replay and structured outputs. This release omits the KV-cache system, prefill/decode disaggregation and cross-replica load balancing, so it is closer to offline inference plus core serving pieces than the full production stack.

**Why it matters.** Cloud vendors and self-hosters can run DeepSeek models with the lab's own serving code, which narrows the performance and cost gap to the official API, and Ascend support gives Huawei's chips a first-party software path.

**Key numbers.** Licence: Apache-2.0 (framework), MIT reported for deepseek-recipe · Hardware: Nvidia SM100, Huawei Ascend 950 · Self-reported by DeepSeek: 155 trillion tokens/day on one cluster, about $38 revenue per card-hour (Chinese-media report, unverified) · HN 2,004 points

*Note: README read directly via GitHub fetch (models, hardware, features, exclusions, Apache-2.0, no release date). The deepseek-recipe MIT licence and the 155T tokens/day and $38 per card-hour figures come only from search snippets of Chinese media; not seen in README text, unverified. 'Fully open' wording in Chinese media overstates scope given the README's omissions.*

### 2 · DHS order bars DeepSeek from federal civilian agencies; China's foreign ministry objects

`I = 71.7` · Policy & safety · China · DeepSeek, US Department of Homeland Security, China Ministry of Foreign Affairs

> 原标题：美国政府指示各机构停用DeepSeek，外交部：反对将科技经贸问题政治化

The US Department of Homeland Security, under the 2018 Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act, ordered civilian federal agencies to stop buying and to remove DeepSeek products, including third-party products built on DeepSeek. The order was made public on 19 Oct US time. Agencies have a 60-day window to request exceptions via CISA. At the 20 Oct briefing, spokesperson Mao Ning said China opposes overstretching the national-security concept and politicising tech and trade issues, and will defend Chinese firms' lawful rights. The order does not bar private-sector use.

**Why it matters.** It is the first formal supply-chain exclusion of a Chinese frontier lab, reaching contractors and anyone embedding DeepSeek in products sold to government, and it raises the cost of open-weight Chinese models in US procurement.

**Key numbers.** 60-day exception window via CISA · Exclusion and Removal Order #2026-01 (per Chinese media) · Scope: civilian agencies only

*Note: Facts from search snippets only (pages not fetchable). Outlets disagree on when the order was signed (CyberScoop says June 26; Yahoo says July 24; Gizmodo cites a July memo made public Monday); the 'disclosed 19 Oct' timing is consistent across sources.
