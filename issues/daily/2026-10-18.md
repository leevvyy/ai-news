### 1 · Anthropic to commit 5% of its shares to new public benefit fund ahead of IPO, pending stockholder approval

`I = 81.2` · Business & funding · US · Anthropic

Anthropic announced it will set aside 5% of its shares for a new public benefit fund structured as a wholly owned LLC, subject to stockholder approval. The stake could grow by an extra 2% at the board's discretion for each 50% rise in company value above an undisclosed baseline. Reuters reports that, counting earlier pledges, roughly 20% of shares would be dedicated to public-purpose uses; Bloomberg frames the fund as a vehicle for donations including mitigating AI's labour-market effects. Anthropic has not named recipients or said how stock would be converted to cash.

**Why it matters.** An unusually large equity pledge by an AI lab weeks before an expected IPO could shape governance, dilution and public scrutiny of the listing.

**Key numbers.** 5% of shares · +2% per 50% value rise above baseline · ~20% of shares for public good incl. earlier pledges

### 2 · Microsoft pulls out of Nscale's Loughton UK data centre deal, Telegraph reports; grid power not expected until 2030s

`I = 66.3` · Compute & chips · Europe · Nscale, Microsoft · `MSFT`

The Telegraph reported, and Reuters and Bloomberg relayed, that Microsoft has withdrawn from its planned anchor-tenant deal for Nscale's data centre in Loughton, Essex. Grid officials had reportedly told Nscale not to expect power until the 2030s. Nscale said its Microsoft relationship 'remains strong' and that it is exploring alternative power for the site. Microsoft declined to comment. Nscale's IPO filing reportedly shows Microsoft and Anthropic make up about 85% of its contracts, and it is seeking up to $3B in a US listing as soon as this month.

**Why it matters.** A concrete case of grid constraints killing a hyperscale deal, hitting a neocloud just ahead of its IPO.

**Key numbers.** Grid power not expected until 2030s · Microsoft + Anthropic ~85% of Nscale contracts · IPO raise up to $3B

*Note: Based on search-result summaries; articles not opened. Reuters copies say Microsoft 'terminated' the agreement, Bloomberg-derived ones say it pulled out, so legal status is unclear. The original Telegraph report was not seen directly.*

### 4 · Samsung to use TSMC 3nm for base dies in Nvidia's next-gen custom HBM, Seoul Economic Daily reports

`I = 58.3` · Compute & chips · Other · Samsung Electronics, TSMC, Nvidia · `005930.KS` `2330.TW` `NVDA`

Per Seoul Economic Daily via Reuters, Samsung will make base dies for Nvidia's next-generation custom HBM on TSMC's 3nm process, reportedly the first time Samsung has worked with TSMC at the HBM manufacturing stage. Nvidia reportedly asked Samsung, SK hynix and Micron to use TSMC's process for base dies and sixth-generation DRAM for the core dies. Samsung reportedly accepted but will keep in-house base dies for standard HBM and other customers' custom parts, and is weighing its own foundry as overflow if TSMC capacity is short.

**Why it matters.** Signals TSMC's growing grip on the HBM stack and a pragmatic Samsung-Nvidia accommodation in memory.

**Key numbers.** TSMC 3nm base die · 6th-gen DRAM core dies

*Note: Press report of a Seoul newspaper relayed by Reuters; no company confirmation. Details seen only in search-result summaries. TrendForce (Oct 9) had already said Samsung and Micron may use TSMC base dies.*

### 5 · Anthropic report: junior share of technical staff fell from 21% to 14%; agents now outwork humans

`I = 57.3` · Research · US · Anthropic

Anthropic's economics team published a study of its own workforce. Junior employees fell from 21% to 14% of technical staff, and juniors' share of technical hires fell from 27% to 9%. By March 2026 combined agent work hours passed human hours, and by August interactive agents logged about six times their handlers' hours. Commits per engineer were about 5x their level six months earlier, with no difference in later reverts among 192 adopters vs 74 non-adopters.

**Why it matters.** A rare first-party dataset on how agentic coding reshapes a frontier lab's hiring and output, with human attention (about 80% of interactive session time spent waiting on people) as the main constraint.

**Key numbers.** Junior share of technical staff 21% to 14% · Junior share of technical hires 27% to 9% · Interactive agent hours ~6x human hours (Aug) · Commits per engineer ~5x in six months · Median meeting time +20% YoY

*Note: Numbers from the Anthropic page, which the desk fetched and read. The page may be dated Oct 16; TechCrunch (Oct 17 20:21Z, ~1.5h before the window) and the HN surge on Oct 18 put it in range. TechCrunch seen only as a harvest headline. The report notes adoption was not random, so productivity estimates may be biased.*

**Sources.** [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic) <sup>primary</sup> · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/17/anthropic-says-its-workforce-has-become-less-junior-and-is-spending-more-time-in-meetings/) <sup>press</sup>
