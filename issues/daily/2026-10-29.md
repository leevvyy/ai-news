### L1 · Read free cash flow as OCF minus capex, and size the funding runway

`Domain AI (finance, health)` · now 3.5 → target 4.5 · priority 0.70 · ~10 min

**Why today.** The item reports AWS growth of 39% alongside a ~$220B capex plan and sharply lower trailing free cash flow, which is the OCF-versus-capex squeeze in practice. ([AWS grows 39% to $46B, fastest in nearly five years, but Amazon misses on EPS and revenue and shares fall after hours](https://ir.aboutamazon.com/news-release/news-release-details/2026/Amazon-com-Announces-Third-Quarter-Results/default.aspx))

Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital expenditure; it is what remains to repay debt, buy back stock or pay dividends. When capex outgrows operating cash flow, FCF turns negative and the gap must be funded by cash on hand, debt or equity. Trailing-twelve-month FCF lags capex ramps because spending hits immediately while the revenue it enables arrives later, so a growth segment can look strong while FCF falls. A simple runway model compounds debt at an interest rate and adds the annual shortfall. It breaks when OCF grows, capex is leased or off-balance-sheet, or timing of payables distorts a single year. The numbers below are illustrative, not Amazon's.

$$
D_{t+1} = D_t (1+r) - \mathrm{FCF},\quad \mathrm{FCF} = \mathrm{OCF} - \mathrm{CapEx}
$$

```python
# Illustrative numbers only (not Amazon's actual figures), in $B per year.
ocf = 140.0          # operating cash flow
capex = 220.0        # capital expenditure
fcf = ocf - capex
print("FCF:", fcf)
# Capex funded by debt: how many years of negative FCF before net debt reaches a limit?
debt, limit, rate = 0.0, 300.0, 0.05
for year in range(1, 11):
    debt = debt * (1 + rate) - fcf
    print(year, round(debt, 1))
    if debt > limit:
        print("limit hit in year", year); break
```
