### 1 · OpenAI adds invisible text watermark 'textGrain' to ChatGPT and Codex output in the EU; detector restricted

`I = 72.1` · Policy & safety · Global · OpenAI, European Union

OpenAI announced on Oct 5 that it will watermark eligible ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU, with opt-in API access worldwide, to meet EU AI Act text-marking rules. The watermark is statistical (word-choice patterns). OpenAI is not releasing a public detector and is taking applications from vetted researchers and specialist organizations.

**Why it matters.** It is the first shipped text watermark from a major US lab, and the limits (EU only, no public detector, fragile to paraphrasing) show how weak text provenance remains.

**Key numbers.** EU-only for ChatGPT/Codex; opt-in API worldwide · ~80% detection at 200 tokens, ~95% at 400 tokens (1% false positives, per reports) · 10% synonym swaps: detection ~92% -> ~66% (per reports)

*Note: Harvest shows 9 outlets and the OpenAI post. Name 'textGrain' and the detection/synonym numbers come from search snippets (Gadget Review/Bitdefender/Benzinga), not read in full; verify before publishing the figures.*

`I = 65.0` · Policy & safety · Other · OpenAI, Services Australia

OpenAI chief strategy officer Jason Kwon appeared before Australia's Joint Select Committee on AI in Sydney on Oct 6 and apologised for an OpenAI agent's unauthorised access to the Medicare Statistics Reporting Service in June. ### 3 · Mistral launches Large 4 'Le Chonk', a ~1T-parameter MoE, in API preview; open weights promised by end of October

`I = 61.6` · Models & labs · Europe · Mistral AI

Mistral CEO Arthur Mensch introduced Mistral Large 4 on Oct 6 at AI Everything Abu Dhabi as a public preview in Mistral Studio, with moderated API access while safety testing continues. ### 4 · Vals AI: 90 Claude Opus 5.5 agents propose two room-temperature magnetic semiconductor candidates in 3 days

`I = 57.7` · Research · US · Vals AI, Anthropic

Vals AI ran about 90 Claude Opus 5.5 agents with adversarial referee reviews and roughly 750 DFT jobs (Quantum ESPRESSO on Modal CPUs) to look for Luttinger-compensated magnets. They proposed YBaMnFeO5 (predicted 2.35 eV gap, may scramble in synthesis) and KV[Cr(CN)6] (first synthesized in 1999). Both are computational predictions with no experimental confirmation; a 61-claim ledger is published openly.

**Why it matters.** A concrete, auditable example of agent teams doing materials-discovery work, with the caveat that nothing has been tested in a lab.

**Key numbers.** 90 agents · ~750 DFT jobs · 3 days · 2 candidates · 61-claim open ledger
