### 1 · UK commits £100M to an AI Biosecurity Lab backed by OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind

`I = 65.6` · Policy & safety · Europe · UK Government, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind

The UK government announced £100M for a new AI Biosecurity Lab, which Bloomberg says will be supported by OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind as part of measures to improve the government's ability to understand and tackle AI risks. Trade outlets describe it as an independent non-profit modelled on the AI Security Institute, with the labs' contributions largely in compute and model credits (about £100M each from OpenAI and up to £100M from Anthropic, per TECHi). ### 2 · Google's privacy policy adds an exception that could let personal-AI data inform ad targeting

`I = 57.6` · Policy & safety · US · Google · `GOOGL`

The Verge reports that Google's updated privacy policy adds an exception to its long-standing promise not to use Drive, Gmail or Google Photos for personalised ads, applying to people who use personalised AI features. Techlicious says the policy, updated Oct 16, still states ads are not personalised directly from Gmail or Photos but that Search may process data users allow it to access to personalise content, including ads. Google says Personal Intelligence is opt-in and that Photos data is not used for ads; Fast Company argues the wording does not rule out future ads informed by Gmail data.

**Why it matters.** It tests how far consumers' trust in opt-in AI assistants holds once ad targeting is on the table, and may draw regulator attention.

**Key numbers.** Policy updated Oct 16 · Personal Intelligence is opt-in

### 4 · Google ordered to halt Finnish data-center site work after clearing an estimated 250,000 trees before environmental review

`I = 54.1` · Compute & chips · Europe · Google, Tuike Finland · `GOOGL`

Fortune reports that Finland's Licensing and Supervision Agency ordered Google subsidiary Tuike Finland to suspend preparatory work at its Muhos and Kajaani sites until environmental impact assessments are finished. About 330 ha was cleared at Muhos and about 200 ha at Kajaani, with Fortune estimating 250,000 trees felled. The New York Post (Oct 24) adds that residents say wolves have entered villages. Google denies illegal logging and says the work met the Forestry Act.

**Why it matters.** A permitting and ESG setback for Google's largest European data-centre investment, adding to the siting backlash against AI infrastructure.

**Key numbers.** ~250,000 trees (Fortune estimate) · ~330 ha Muhos, ~200 ha Kajaani · €13B Finnish investment

*Note: Fortune ran on Oct 23, before the window; published_at is the New York Post time from the harvest. Tree counts conflict (Fortune ~250,000, Cybernews headline 400,000 across two sites); the hectare figures are more consistent. Article bodies were not fetched; details are from search summaries. 'Illegal' is the NY Post framing and Google disputes it.*

**Sources.** [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/23/google-finland-data-center-250-000-trees-environment/) <sup>press</sup> · [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/10/24/business/google-illegally-tore-down-250k-trees-for-finland-data-center-infuriating-locals-report/) <sup>press</sup> · [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/google-finland-data-center-trees/) <sup>trade</sup>
