### L1 · Estimate decode throughput from memory bandwidth, then size a fleet

`LLM internals & post-training` · now 2.5 → target 4.5 · priority 2.00 · ~15 min

**Why today.** The CCID item cites 175 trillion daily token calls, a volume you can turn into a rough accelerator count once you know what limits decoding. ([China's MIIT think tank CCID says AI inference data now exceeds training data; reports 175T+ daily tokens](https://www.ithome.com/1/020/809.htm))

Autoregressive decoding reads every weight once per step, so each step costs about (parameter bytes)/(memory bandwidth) regardless of how many sequences share it. Batching B sequences therefore multiplies throughput by B until compute or KV-cache traffic becomes the bottleneck. Dividing a daily token volume by 86,400 gives tokens per second, and dividing by per-accelerator throughput gives a fleet size. It breaks for long contexts (KV reads grow with length), MoE models (only active experts are read, but batching touches more of them) and speculative decoding. All figures below are illustrative, not any real model or chip.

$$
t_{step} \approx \frac{P\, b_w}{BW},\quad \text{tok/s} \approx \frac{B}{t_{step}}
$$

```python
# Illustrative numbers only: decode is memory-bandwidth bound.
params = 70e9          # parameters
bytes_per = 1          # 8-bit weights
bw = 3.35e12           # bytes/s HBM bandwidth (illustrative accelerator)
batch = 64             # sequences decoded together; weights are read once per step
step_s = params*bytes_per/bw
tok_s = batch/step_s
print(f"step {step_s*1e3:.1f} ms, {tok_s:.0f} tokens/s per accelerator")
target = 175e12/86400  # tokens/s implied by 175T tokens/day
print(f"accelerators for 175T/day at this rate: {target/tok_s:,.0f}")
```
