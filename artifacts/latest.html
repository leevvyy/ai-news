Components and weights

Computed components

Impact and novelty are rubric judgements made during research. The other three are computed in Python from the sources and the archive, so they cannot drift with the writer's mood.

scred=1−∏j≤n(1−rtier(j)) sbreadth=1−e−c/κ smomentum=1−e−mt/(μ7+1)
  • Credibility is a noisy-OR over the n = most reliable distinct outlet domains j: the probability that at least one of them is reliable, with tier reliabilities . Capping n stops a pile of links from saturating it.
  • Breadth saturates with c, the number of independent outlets covering the story (κ = ).
  • Momentum is an attention burst: mt is the number of stories in this issue on the item's primary entity, set against μ7, that entity's mean stories per day over the previous archived days. Four stories on a usually quiet lab score near 1; a lab that is in the news every day needs a bigger burst.

Placement

Items are sorted by I, ties broken by credibility. The first verified items become lead stories (verified means at least one primary source, or at least distinct non-social outlets), with at most leads per primary entity so one company cannot fill the page. The next become briefs, and everything else is kept as "also noted" for the weekly pool.

The "Entities in play" boards weight each story's score fully to its primary entity and by 0.25 to every other entity it names.

Worked example

Market read-through

For each ticker a story names, day 0 is the first session of that stock's exchange whose close falls after the story was published. Returns are measured against the stock's home index (S&P 500, KOSPI, Nikkei 225, TAIEX, Hang Seng or CSI 300) with a market model fitted by OLS over 60 sessions ending 10 sessions before day 0:

ARt=Ri,t−(α^+β^Rm,t),CAR=∑t=02ARt,t=CARσ^n

Chips turn accent-coloured only when |t| ≥ . Several stories can hit the same stock on the same day and n is small, so read these as descriptive context, never as a trading signal. The weekly recap averages CAR by topic with a 95% Student-t interval.

China desk

At least stories from China appear among the leads and briefs. When the ranking alone would show fewer, the best remaining China stories (verified first) replace the lowest-scoring non-China briefs and are marked "quota pick", so the swap stays visible.

Window and dedupe

Each run covers the time since the previous run's window closed (24 h on a normal day, capped at 72 h after missed runs) and is dated by the reader-local date of the window's midpoint. A story is a duplicate of anything in the previous 7 days when it shares a canonical source URL, or when its title's token Jaccard overlap is at least 0.5 with a shared entity. Genuine developments carry a follow_up_of link instead.
